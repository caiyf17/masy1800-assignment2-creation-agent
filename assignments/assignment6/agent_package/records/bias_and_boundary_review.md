# Advocate and risk-officer challenge

The advocate’s strongest argument is that delaying every reversible experiment can postpone valuable learning. That argument supports a bounded public/synthetic PoC when the scope is permitted and the organization values early learning. It does not demonstrate operational benefit.

The risk officer’s strongest argument is that fast drafting hides review, correction, maintenance and disclosure costs. That argument supports action-specific permissions, the best adequate comparator and clear stops. It does not justify rejecting every low-consequence learning option forever.

The synthesis resists novelty and fear-of-missing-out by withholding automatic adoption from broad survey use. It resists sunk-cost bias by excluding the completed learning exercise from prospective H3 costs. It resists excessive conservatism through the authorized PoC contrast and positive experiment/adoption controls. Risk preference changes sequencing, not evidence or minimum safeguards.

Handoff to Organizational Adoption: can the organization actually staff, govern, fund, train, support, change and sustain the selected scope? This agent requests or consumes that assessment rather than certifying readiness. Legal/data/vendor terms and actual operating permission require qualified human decisions; no company data was accessed and no deployment was approved by this academic work.
