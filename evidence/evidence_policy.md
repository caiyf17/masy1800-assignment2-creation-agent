# Evidence Policy

- Prefer original research papers, standards bodies, official repositories, and dated first-party product documentation for origin, architecture, and release claims.
- Record publication or release dates and the date a time-sensitive web source was accessed.
- Label synthesis and causal interpretation as inference. A chronological sequence alone does not prove that one development caused another.
- Do not treat vendor statements, model memory, generated summaries, benchmark marketing, or fluent prose as independent proof of production reliability or organizational value.
- For current platform status, recheck official documentation before reuse because agent frameworks, APIs, pricing, and availability change quickly.
- When sources conflict, preserve the conflict and lower confidence. When a consequential claim lacks adequate support, abstain or request more information.
- Keep recommendations bounded to technology-creation implications. Defer diffusion, adoption readiness, overall risk acceptance, and implementation approval to the appropriate specialists and human decision owners.
